The Mustache Files · Let's do mustache stuff.

Competitive Mustaching Is a Real Sport. There Are Judges.

There are championships, governing organizations, carefully defined categories, and actual adults enforcing restrictions on the angle of a mustache.

The Institute assumed competitive mustaching was an informal gathering of proud gentlemen. We were wrong. It is an international activity with rulebooks. A mustache can be disqualified from the wrong category. We have entered a new administrative era.

Finding 01: The world championships exist

The World Beard and Moustache Championships bring together competitors displaying mustaches, partial beards and full beards. The World Beard and Moustache Association helps coordinate international competition. Organized modern contests have roots in European facial-hair clubs, with significant events taking shape by 1990.

These are judged style competitions, not simply contests to determine whose facial hair is longest. Presentation, category compliance, shape and grooming matter.

Finding 02: There is more than one kind of mustache

Competition categories can include natural mustaches, English mustaches with long outward extensions, styled handlebars, slender upward-pointing Dalí mustaches, and freestyle constructions. Exact names and requirements vary by event and rulebook.

Some categories permit styling products. Natural categories restrict them. Freestyle does not mean that a competitor can install a concealed wire frame and call it facial hair. In this sport, structural assistance has limits.

Finding 03: The rules are gloriously specific

For the 2027 world championship, published rules include restrictions on how far the tips of a Dalí mustache may rise. Other categories regulate which facial hairs may contribute to the design. This is the sort of specificity normally associated with aviation or municipal zoning.

Imagine telling a competitor that his mustache has exceeded the eyebrow boundary. Now imagine being the person with the authority to say it.

Finding 04: America participates enthusiastically

The United States hosts National Beard and Moustache Championships, including a scheduled 2026 event in Anchorage, Alaska. There are numerous classes and a Best in Show award. The 2003 world championship in Carson City, Nevada, helped introduce organized international competition to American audiences.

The 2017 world event in Austin reported hundreds of competitors from dozens of countries. This is not a small gathering behind a barbershop. It is a genuine international subculture.

Finding 05: What are judges actually judging?

Not hygiene, generally. Judges evaluate the facial hair according to the category's published standards, including style, craftsmanship, presentation and rule compliance. Cleanliness still matters to the owner and anyone close enough to encounter the mustache, but the Institute found no official international soup-resistance category.

We believe this is an oversight.

THE MEISTER'S VERDICT

Competitive mustaching is real. It has rules, judges, and jurisdictional disputes over upper-lip territory. We support the athletes. We request a soup trial.
