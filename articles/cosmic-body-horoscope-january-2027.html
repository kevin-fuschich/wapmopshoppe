Cycle Almanac: January's Moisture Report

The celestial metaphor is entertaining. Moisture has actual biological context. Cervical mucus changes across many menstrual cycles; vaginal lubrication and vulvar skin comfort are related but distinct questions. These observations are not tied to the moon or zodiac.

☾ Menstruation · A Different Environment

Bleeding and menstrual products may change how things feel. Avoid perfumed products if they irritate your skin; seek care for unusually heavy bleeding or significant pain.

◐ Follicular Phase · Changing Mucus

Cervical mucus may become more abundant or wetter as ovulation approaches, although patterns differ from person to person.

◯ Around Ovulation · Slippery Conditions

Some people notice clear, stretchy or slippery cervical mucus. This observation alone is not reliable contraception.

◑ Luteal Phase · Another Set of Conditions

Discharge can change after ovulation. Persistent vaginal dryness, painful sex or irritation should not automatically be dismissed as “just your cycle.”

Departmental distinction: Cervical mucus, vaginal lubrication, and vulvar moisture are not interchangeable. Hormonal changes, medicines and life stages may affect comfort. Moon phases and zodiac signs do not predict them. A clinician can help evaluate persistent dryness, burning or pain.

The January Equipment Advisory

The practical recommendation for January is wonderfully uncommercial: don't buy a reset kit for an organ that regulates itself. Avoid douching and internal deodorants. Clean the external vulva gently. If dryness or friction is troubling you, consider an appropriate lubricant and get ongoing symptoms assessed rather than assuming a moisturizer or horoscope is the solution.

The Department of Earthly Evidence has issued its January finding: start with observation, not a shopping cart.
