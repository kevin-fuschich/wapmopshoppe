The Great Mustache Disappearances

Some men changed their appearance. Others caused a national incident.

There are mustaches, and then there are public institutions attached to upper lips. When one of those disappears, the response can resemble a small-scale cultural emergency. The Institute reviewed three documented cases.

Case 01: Alex Trebek, 2001

Classification: Unauthorized removal of a national landmark.

After years of hosting Jeopardy! with a mustache, Trebek removed it between tapings in 2001. He later told Larry King that the decision was spontaneous: he went to the makeup room, asked for clippers, and took it off before the day's final show. The change made national news. He brought the mustache back briefly in 2014.

Case 02: Burt Reynolds, 1978

Classification: Upper-lip demolition under peer pressure.

On the September 25, 1978, episode of The Tonight Show, guest host Steve Martin challenged Reynolds to shave his trademark mustache. Reynolds obtained a razor and began removing it on television. This is not an urban legend: contemporary reporting and a UPI photograph document the event. A later photograph even shows Reynolds clean-shaven while wax-museum staff wore fake mustaches around him. The mustache eventually returned.

Case 03: Tom Selleck, periodically

Classification: Temporary dismantling for professional purposes.

Tom Selleck's mustache is so recognizable that appearing without it can feel like a continuity error. He has shaved it for acting work, including his role as Dwight Eisenhower in Ike: Countdown to D-Day. Unlike Trebek's impulsive removal or Reynolds' televised challenge, Selleck's clean-shaven appearances have often been dictated by the job.

Finding: A mustache can outgrow its owner

When a distinctive mustache becomes part of someone's public identity, its removal can be surprisingly newsworthy. That does not make the hair public property. It does, however, mean the upper lip has acquired an audience.

THE MEISTER'S VERDICT

The Institute has determined that a mustache may legally belong to its owner but emotionally belong to millions of strangers. All removals should remain voluntary. Advance public notice is appreciated but not required.
